Normative myelin water imaging (MWI) atlases were developed to characterize spatial patterns and population-level variation in myelin distribution. These tools support voxel-wise analysis of individual data and enhance interpretation of myelin-related changes in neurological disorders.

---

## 🔸 Myelin Water Atlas of the Human Brain
A normative myelin water atlas was generated by averaging MWF maps from 50 healthy adults acquired at 3T. The atlas provides voxel-wise mean and variability of MWF in standard MNI space and serves as a reference to detect regional demyelination in individuals.

**Highlights:**
- Based on 3D GRASE multi-echo T2 acquisition
- Significant regional variation in MWF observed across white matter
- High anatomical specificity: corpus callosum splenium > genu
- Applied to detect abnormal myelin loss in MS patients (z < −1.96)

## 🔸 Myelin Water and T2 Atlas of the Cervical Spinal Cord
Two quantitative atlases of the cervical spinal cord were developed from 20 healthy participants: a Myelin Water Fraction (MWF) atlas and an intra-/extracellular geometric mean T2 (IEGMT2) atlas.

**Highlights:**
- Developed using 3D GRASE multi-echo T2 imaging at 3T
- Reflects known anatomical features: dorsal column, lateral funiculi
- Enables patient-specific z-score mapping of myelin loss and water mobility changes
- IEGMT2 complements MWF by revealing edema-like tissue properties

## 💡 Impact
The atlases offer high-resolution normative references for quantifying myelin and tissue water properties in the brain and spinal cord. Their application facilitates individualized assessment in demyelinating diseases and supports research into microstructural integrity across populations.
