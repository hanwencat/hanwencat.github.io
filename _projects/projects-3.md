This project applies myelin water imaging (MWI) to investigate microstructural spinal cord pathology in individuals with cervical spondylotic myelopathy (CSM). By integrating advanced MRI and neurophysiological assessments, this study demonstrates the added diagnostic value of MWI in detecting demyelination that may not be visible on conventional imaging.

---

## 🔸 Myelin Water Imaging Application in Cervical Spondylotic Myelopathy

To evaluate whether myelin water fraction (MWF), derived from multi-echo T2 relaxation MRI, can detect spinal cord demyelination in patients with CSM, and how these findings correlate with somatosensory evoked potentials (SSEPs) and standard MRI indices of compression.

---

**Highlights:**
- MWI was applied to assess myelin content in the cervical spinal cord of CSM patients and healthy controls.
- Conventional MRI markers (e.g., MSCC, MCC, T2 hyperintensity) failed to correlate with functional deficits.
- Reduced MWF was observed specifically in CSM patients with pathologic SSEPs, indicating functional demyelination.
- Significant correlations were found between MWF and SSEP latencies only in the CSM group.
- The study demonstrates the value of combining myelin-specific imaging with electrophysiology to detect clinically meaningful spinal cord pathology.

## 💡 Impact

This study provides in vivo evidence that demyelination occurs in CSM and can be captured using MWI. By combining MWI with neurophysiological markers, improved diagnostic specificity may be achieved for patients with ambiguous clinical or imaging presentations. These insights support the use of MWF as a clinically relevant biomarker in spinal cord compression syndromes.
